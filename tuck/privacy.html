Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 10, 2026

Tuck is a clipboard manager for Mac made by SP Digital Labs, the name under which Joseph Suzuki-Parker, an independent developer based in Japan, publishes apps ("we", "us"). This policy explains what Tuck keeps, where it keeps it, and the choices you have.

The short version: Tuck collects nothing. It has no account, no sync, no analytics and no ads, and it never connects to the internet. Your clipboard history is stored only on your Mac, and you can delete it at any time.

Information we collect

None. Tuck does not send anything to us or to anyone else. We cannot see what you copy, how you use the app, or that you use it at all.

What Tuck keeps on your Mac

  • Your clipboard history: the text, file paths and images you copy, with the name of the app each came from and the time.
  • Screenshots: if you choose a screenshot folder in Settings, new screenshots saved there are added to the history. Tuck can only read the folder you choose, and only reads it.
  • Text in images: Tuck recognizes the text in images so you can search for them. This is done on your Mac by macOS. The image is not sent anywhere.
  • Your settings: your shortcut, layout, history limits and the list of apps Tuck should never record from.

All of this is stored in Tuck's own folder on your Mac. It is not uploaded, backed up to us, or shared between your devices. If you back up your Mac, for example with Time Machine, the history is included in that backup like your other files.

Passwords and other secrets

  • Anything a password manager marks as hidden is skipped and never recorded.
  • Copies made in the apps listed under Settings > Privacy are not recorded. Common password managers are on that list from the start, and you can add any app.
  • Text that looks like a password, API key or access token is held in memory only. It is shown masked, is never written to disk, and is forgotten after 10 minutes or when Tuck quits.
  • Text recognized in an image is not kept if it looks like a secret.

This detection is a safeguard, not a guarantee. A secret in an unusual form may be recorded like any other text, so add the apps you copy sensitive things from to the list.

Permissions Tuck asks for

  • Accessibility: used only to press Cmd+V for you, so the clip you pick is pasted into the app you were in. Tuck does not read your screen or what you type. Without this permission, Tuck puts the clip on the clipboard and you paste it yourself.
  • A folder you choose: used only to notice new screenshots.

How long it is kept

Tuck keeps the most recent clips up to the limits in Settings > History (1,000 clips and 200 images unless you change them) and drops older ones. Clips you pin are kept until you unpin or delete them.

Deleting your data

  • Delete one clip by selecting it and pressing Cmd+Delete.
  • Delete the history with Settings > History > Clear History. Pinned clips are kept; delete those one by one.
  • Deleting Tuck and its data from your Mac removes everything. There is nothing for us to delete, because we never had it.

Who we share it with

No one. We don't have your data, we don't sell data, and Tuck contains no third-party analytics or advertising code.

If you have chosen to share diagnostics with app developers in macOS, Apple may give us crash reports and usage statistics that do not identify you. You can turn this off in System Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements.

Children

Tuck collects no information from anyone, including children.

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we will update the date at the top.
